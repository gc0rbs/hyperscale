# Your first rig

A rig is one deposit. You can own as many rigs as you like, and opening another rig is the intended way to add capital, because a rig's stake is fixed once it is activated.

## Activate

1. Open **Mine**, then **Activate a rig**.
2. Choose the amount of $RIG to stake. The minimum stake weight is set per season (default **100 RIG**).
3. Review the preview. It shows the stake, the activation fee, and your rig's starting hashrate.
4. Approve $RIG, then confirm the activation.

| Item | Amount (defaults) | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stake | Your choice, at least the minimum | Held by the mine, **returned in full** at close (or minus 3% on early exit) |
| Activation fee | 1% of the stake, charged on top | Treasury |
| Starting hashrate | Equal to your stake (GPU tier 0 is 1.0×) | |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Why the stake is fixed.** Upgrade prices are a percentage of your rig's stake. If stake could be added later, someone would buy every tier on a tiny rig and then top it up. So the stake is immutable and the price of every upgrade is the same *relative* price for everyone.
{% endhint %}

## Before the mine opens

A season can open the moment it is created, or after a short pre-open. During pre-open you can activate rigs and buy GPU and cooling tiers, but nothing works yet and overclocks are not available. A GPU tier bought in pre-open boosts 100% of the season's work, which is the cheapest hashrate you will ever buy.

## Once it is open

Your rig works every second from the moment the mine opens or the moment you activate, whichever is later. The mine page shows:

* your rig's hashrate, heat and active overclocks,
* the current block, its progress bar and the shift counter (8 shifts per block),
* estimated time to the next shift, the block being found, and close,
* your live fragment estimate for the current block.

You can close the browser. Work is credited on chain by the clock, not by your session. Come back to claim, to buy upgrades, or to re-buy overclocks.

## Two rigs or one big rig?

Pay, upgrade prices and overclocks are all linear in stake, so splitting a stake across rigs gains nothing and costs an extra activation fee. The reasons to run more than one rig are adding capital later, or running different strategies (one rig upgraded early and left alone, one rig overclocked in the finale).
